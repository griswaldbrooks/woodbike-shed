# Stage 8 — Rafters

> **Goal:** thirteen 2×6 rafters seated in their birdsmouths on both double top plates,
> defining the roof plane.
> **Crew:** 2 people for the raising (1 alone can cut) · **Time:** a full day ·
> **Weather:** dry and calm — 10-foot boards catch the wind

This is the page where expensive stock gets ruined or saved. Every rafter is identical, so
you cut **one pattern rafter first, prove it fits both plates, and only then mark the other
twelve from it.** Nothing gets cut in anger until the pattern sits right.

The roof is a single slope — there is no ridge board. Every rafter runs downhill from the
front (high) wall to the back (low) wall, at **4½ in 12** (it falls 4½" per foot of run).

## Before you start

Parts for this page:

| Qty | Part | Lumber | Cut length |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13 | rafter | 2×6 KD | 9' 7⅜" |

Buy or cut your stock with a little to spare: fourteen blanks is not greed if the lumber
yard lets you pick through the pile. Choose the straightest board for the pattern.

Tools: tape, pencil, speed square, circular saw, handsaw, sawhorses, clamps, 4' level,
two ladders, a helper. Print the birdsmouth template (Fig 11.3 below) at full size before
you start cutting.

Already built: walls standing and braced; rake plates and stepped studs in
([Stage 7](10-rake-plates.md)).

## Steps

### 1. Mark the rafter layout on both double top plates

Thirteen rafters at **15⅞" on centre**. Hook your tape on the left end of the front double
top plate and mark the rafter centres: the first at **¾"**, then every 15⅞" — 16⅝", 32½",
48⅜", 64¼", 80⅛", 96", 111⅞", 127¾", 143⅝", 159½", 175⅜", out to **191¼"** at the right
end. The first rafter sits over the left rake wall, the last over the right.

Mark the same centres on the back double top plate. The back plate starts 3½" inboard of
the front plate's left end, so each tape reading drops by 3½" — and the first rafter's back
seat lands on the left side wall's double top plate, which runs over the back wall just to
catch it. The right end works the same way.

![Fig 11.1 — Rafter layout plan at wall-plate level, looking down, Stage 8. Thirteen rafter centres at 15⅞" on centre across both double top plates, first over the left rake wall, last over the right. Layout marks are made on the plates before any rafter is cut.](figures/fig-11-layout-plan.svg)

### 2. Cut the pattern rafter's ends and birdsmouths

Take the straightest 2×6 and mark its top edge — the edge that will face the sky. Crown the
board the same way as the wall studs if it has one. Cut one end square; that plumb end is
the **front tail**. Working along the top edge from that end, make three marks:

1. **25⅛"** — the front heel, where the front birdsmouth starts.
2. another **73¾"** — the back seat start, where the back birdsmouth starts.
3. another **16½"** — that is the back end. The three marks add up to exactly 9' 7⅜", so
   the last one should land on the board's end; if it does not, find the mistake before
   cutting anything.

At the front heel, use the template (Fig 11.3): its underside line rides the rafter's bottom
edge, heel to heel. Trace the **level seat, 4" long**, and the **plumb kick, 1½" deep**, at
the seat's inboard end. At the back seat start, trace the back birdsmouth the same way:
**level seat, 3½" long** (the full plate width), **plumb kick, 1-5/16" deep**, at the seat's
downhill end. Both notches read the same way on the board: seat level, kick plumb, kick to
the right of the seat.

> ⚠️ **WARNING** — Birdsmouth cuts are short crosscuts near your holding hand · blade
> contact and kickback · saw the seat cut first, then the kick cut, keep the kerf on the
> waste side of the line, and let the offcut fall free — never hold it underneath the blade.

Saw each seat cut level, then each kick cut plumb. Stop the seat cut exactly at the kick
line — if you overcut past the corner, the rafter rocks on its seat and that board becomes
a cripple for the shed wall, not a rafter. The finished notch is a clean two-sided corner;
check it against the template.

![Fig 11.2 — Section through one birdsmouthed rafter, viewed from the left gable end, Stage 8. The level front seat lands on the front plate top and the level back seat on the back plate top; plumb kicks stand at the wall faces and plumb tails give the 24" front and 12" back overhangs.](figures/fig-11-rafter-section.svg)

### 3. Test the pattern on the wall — both plates

Carry the pattern rafter up and set it on the layout marks at the left end: seat in front,
seat in back. Check every one of these before cutting another board:

- Both seats bear flat, no rocking, no daylight under either seat.
- The front kick stands tight against the front wall's inner face; the heel stands ½" proud
  of the plate's outer face — that is as designed, do not trim it off.
- The back kick stands tight against the back wall's outer face, seat covering the full
  plate width.
- The top edge touches the rake plate's bearing line at the gable end.
- The plumb tails hang 24" past the front wall face and 12" past the back wall face.

![Fig 11.3 — Birdsmouth template at full size, printed at 100%. The front notch (4" seat, 1½" kick) and the back notch (3½" seat, 1-5/16" kick), both at the roof pitch of 4½ in 12 — the same angle as the rake plate's top edge. Measure the 4" check line before trusting any mark it made.](figures/fig-11-birdsmouth-template.svg)

> ⚠️ **WARNING** — A rafter seated on one plate only will tip under the first load ·
> collapsed rafter, bent stock, fall hazard · before letting go of any rafter, look at BOTH
> seats. If one seat is proud, the notch is wrong — recut from your spare, do not shim a
> structural bearing. See [troubleshooting](16-troubleshooting.md) if a seat gaps.

### 4. Cut the remaining twelve rafters from the pattern

Clamp the pattern to each new board, top edges together, and trace around the tails and both
notches. Cut to the lines, kerf on the waste side. As you go, stack the finished rafters and
sight down their top edges — a twisted blank shows up now, while it is still cheap.

Check each rafter's overall length against the pattern (9' 7⅜") and keep any board that
misses by more than an eighth for a different job.

### 5. Stand the rafters

Two people, one at each end. Stand the first rafter on its marks over the left rake wall
and the last one over the right rake wall, each seated in both birdsmouths, and toe-nail or
screw through each plate into the rafter. Sight along the top edges of those two — they
should read as one straight, even slope. Stand the remaining rafters on their marks in any
order, seating each one in both plates and fastening each one the same way.

> ⚠️ **WARNING** — Working from ladders with 10-foot boards overhead · falls and dropped
> lumber · keep both ladders solid and level, lift the boards up between two people, and
> never work a rafter into place while standing under it.

Until the fascia and roof bracing go on ([Stage 9](12-fascia-and-roof.md)), the rafters can
still tip sideways. Keep them plumb-faced; a brace board nailed diagonally across a few of
them holds the row in line.

## Before you move on

- [ ] Every rafter bears flat on **both** plates — no daylight at either seat, checked by
      sighting from each end.
- [ ] Spacing holds at 15⅞" on centre along both plates — checked with a tape.
- [ ] All thirteen top edges read as one plane — checked by sighting down them and with a
      straight board laid across.
- [ ] Front tails stand 24" past the front wall face, back tails 12" past the back wall
      face — checked with a tape.
- [ ] Rafter faces stand plumb — checked with a 4' level.
