# Stage 3 — Front wall (tall, openings)

> **Goal:** the tall front wall — 10' 3" from deck to plate top — lies assembled flat on the
> deck, with both door openings framed: plates, kings, jacks, headers and cripples all nailed off.
> **Crew:** 1 person (2 makes layout easier) · **Time:** about half a day · **Weather:** dry deck

## Before you start

**Parts** (framing order, cut-list names):

| Qty | Part | Size | Cut length |
|---:|:---|:---|---:|
| 1 | front wall bottom plate | 2×4 | 16' 0" |
| 1 | front wall top plate | 2×4 | 16' 0" |
| 1 | front wall double top plate | 2×4 | 16' 0" |
| 8 | front wall king studs | 2×4 | 9' 10½" |
| 1 | front wall king stud (tall) | 2×4 | 10' 0" |
| 2 | front wall jack studs — opening A | 2×4 | 7' 0" |
| 2 | front wall jack studs — opening B | 2×4 | 6' 10½" |
| 3 | front wall headers — opening B | 2×4 | 6' 3" |
| 3 | front wall headers — opening A | 2×4 | 3' 3" |
| 6 | front wall cripple studs | 2×4 | 2' 7" |

- Deck is done (Stage 2). This wall is framed flat along the deck's **front edge — the street
  side** — bottom plate outer face flush with the deck edge. It goes up in Stage 6.
- All wall lumber is 2×4 KD SPF #2.
- **Fasteners are not modeled and not on either lumber order** — supply your own framing
  fasteners. No wall sheathing either: the lap siding (Stage 10) is the wall surface, applied
  straight over this framing.
- Tools: tape, pencil, speed square, saw, hammer or driver.

## Steps

### 1. Mark the bottom plate

Lay the 16' 0" bottom plate flat on the deck, square with the deck's front edge, outer face flush
with the edge. Hook your tape on the **left end — the end at your left as you face the shed from
the street** — and mark every position in the table. Each mark is the **left edge** of that
member; the member is 1½" wide, running to the right of its mark.

| Tape reading from the left end | Member |
|---:|:---|
| 0" | left-corner king stud — sits on the plate |
| 16" | king stud |
| 17½" | opening A jack stud — **runs to the deck** |
| 55" | opening A jack stud — **runs to the deck** |
| 56½" | tall king stud (10' 0") — **runs to the deck** |
| 64" | king stud |
| 80" | king stud |
| 96" | king stud |
| 99½" | king stud |
| 101" | opening B jack stud — sits on the plate |
| 174½" | opening B jack stud — sits on the plate |
| 176" | king stud |
| 190½" | right-corner king stud — sits on the plate |

<!-- model trace: tape reading = model x + 3.5 -->

### 2. Cut the three deck-notch gaps in the plate

Three studs run to the deck and pass **through** the plate's depth: the two opening-A jacks
(17½" and 55") and the tall king beside the right jack (56½"). Saw out the 1½" plate segment at
each of those three marks so the plate butts against the stud's sides there. Leave the rest of
the plate intact — including the segment across opening A between the two jacks; that threshold
piece keeps the wall square while you frame it and is sawn out only after the wall is raised
(Stage 6).

<!-- model trace: the three through-plate members overlap the plate 7.9 in³ each in the audit —
documented modeling quirk, exempt from the interference sweep (see R02 quirks) -->

### 3. Set the jacks and kings

Set the studs on their marks, all on the same face line of the plate:

- The two 7' 0" opening-A jacks in their notches, bottom on the deck.
- The 10' 0" tall king in its notch, bottom on the deck, tight against the right-hand opening-A
  jack.
- The eight 9' 10½" kings on top of the plate — including both corner studs, flush with the
  plate ends.

Fasten each stud to the plate.

![Fig 6.1 — Front wall framing, elevation viewed from the street, assembled flat (Stage 3). The
full stud map: two door openings, 3' 0" and 6' 0" wide, both heads 7' 0" above the deck.](figures/fig-06-a.svg)

### 4. Build the two headers on their jacks

Each header is **three 2×4s cut to the same length**: two standing on edge, one flush with each
wall face, with the third lying flat across the top of both, capping them. Assembled, the header
is 5" tall and the full 3½" wall depth. Build opening A's header from the three 3' 3" pieces and
opening B's from the three 6' 3" pieces. Set each header on its two jacks, bottom edge **84" up
from the wall's bottom edge — a 7' 0" head over the deck** — flush with the wall faces, and
fasten it to the jacks.

![Fig 6.2 — Header make-up, one door jamb in elevation from the street (Stage 3). Two 2×4s on
edge at the faces plus one flat cap: three boards make the 5" header that seats on the jacks at
the 7' 0" head line.](figures/fig-06-b.svg)

### 5. Add the cripples over the headers

Mark the cripple left edges on the **top plate**, same tape readings from the left end: 32" and
48" over opening A; 112", 128", 144" and 160" over opening B. Set the six 2' 7" cripples
between the header tops and the top-plate marks, and fasten both ends.

### 6. Nail on the top plate, then the double top plate

Lay the top plate over the stud ends, aligned with the bottom plate, and fasten it through into
every stud. Then lay the double top plate on top of it, full width, ends flush. The wall will be
10' 3" tall once it goes up. **Do not raise it yet** — all four walls are raised together, in
order, in Stage 6.

## Before you move on

- [ ] Diagonals of the flat wall equal within ½" — checked with the tape.
- [ ] Both openings are 3' 0" (A) and 6' 0" (B) wide between jack faces — checked with the tape.
- [ ] Header undersides sit 84" up from the wall's bottom edge at both openings — checked with
      the tape.
- [ ] Every stud, jack, cripple and both headers is fastened; the wall lies flat and square on
      the deck, ready to be raised in Stage 6.
