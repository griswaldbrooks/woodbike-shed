# Stage 7 — Rake plates and stepped studs

> **Goal:** each side wall carries a sloped rake plate whose top edge is the roof's bearing
> line, propped on four short stepped studs.
> **Crew:** 1 person · **Time:** about half a day · **Weather:** dry

The roof slopes from the front (high) to the back (low) at **4½ in 12** — it falls 4½" for
every 12" (one foot) of horizontal run. The rake plate's top edge follows that slope: it is
the line the rafters will bear against at the gable ends. Get this line right and the roof
frames itself; get it wrong and every rafter shows it.

## Before you start

Parts for this page (cut-list names; exact lengths on the [cut list](r01-cut-list.md)):

| Qty | Part | Lumber | Cut length |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | left rake wall top plate | 2×4 KD | 5' 9⅜" |
| 1 | right rake wall top plate | 2×4 KD | 5' 9⅜" |
| 4 | left rake wall studs | 2×4 KD | 22¾" · 17" · 11" · 5" |
| 4 | right rake wall studs | 2×4 KD | 22¾" · 17" · 11" · 5" |

The stepped studs are short enough that most come out of the offcut pile from wall framing
(see the board packing on the [cut list](r01-cut-list.md)).

Tools: tape, pencil, speed square (or framing square), circular saw and handsaw, 4' level,
ladder or step, string line or straight board long enough to span the wall.

Already built: all four walls standing, plumb, braced, and tied at the plate line
([Stage 6](09-raise-and-brace.md)). The side walls' double top plates top out
97⅛" above the deck.

## Steps

### 1. Snap the bearing line on each side wall

On the left wall, measure up from the deck at the front end and mark **121½"** on the inside
face of the wall, right where the front wall meets it. At the back end the line lands on the
double top plate top itself, 97⅛" above the deck. Snap or straight-edge a line between the
two marks. Mark the right wall the same way.

Check the middle: 32½" in from the front end, the line should sit **109-5/16"** above the
deck. If it does, the slope is right.

![Fig 10.1 — Left rake wall, elevation viewed from inside the shed, at the start of Stage 7. The finished wall is grey; the new rake plate and four stepped studs are in colour. The plate's top edge is the bearing line: 121½" above the deck at the front end, falling to 97⅛" at the back.](figures/fig-10-rake-wall.svg)

### 2. Cut the rake plates

Take one 5' 9⅜" rake plate and offer it up on the wall: its back end flat on the double top
plate, its top edge on the bearing line. Trim the front nose with a plumb (vertical) cut so
the nose lands flush with the front wall's inner face. Cut the second plate the same way —
the two walls mirror each other.

> ⚠️ **WARNING** — Circular saw cuts at chest height when the plate is held on the wall ·
> kickback and blade contact · support the plate on sawhorses or the deck, never in the air,
> and keep the cut line clear of your free hand.

The plate lies so its wide face follows the roof slope — its top long edge is the roof's edge
line, and the stepped studs will meet its underside.

### 3. Stand the front stud and set the plate

Stand the longest stepped stud — the 22¾" one — plumb at the front end of the wall, bottom on
the double top plate, against the front wall. Its top lands just under the bearing line. Rest
the rake plate on it: nose at the front wall's inner face, back end seated flat on the double
top plate. Brace the plate or have it held, then fasten the plate down through the double top
plate and into the stud top.

### 4. Cut the remaining stepped studs

Cut the three shorter studs per side — 17", 11", and 5" — with their tops sloped to match the
rake plate's underside. That slope is the roof pitch: **4½ in 12**, the same 20.6° angle you
will use for the rafter birdsmouths ([Stage 8](11-rafters.md)) — set your bevel or speed
square once and use it for all eight studs.

The cut-list lengths are exact for the modeled positions; cuts land within an eighth, so cut
each a hair long and sneak up on the fit.

### 5. Fit the stepped studs under the plate

Working front to back, stand each stud plumb on the double top plate at the same layout as
the wall below: stud faces at **15½", 31½", and 47½"** measured from the front end of the
plate line (the first, 22¾" stud already sits tight against the front wall). Toe-fasten each
stud at the bottom, its sloped top tight against the plate underside with no daylight, then
fasten the plate to each stud top.

Repeat steps 1–5 on the right wall. The right wall has a door opening below, but the rake
zone on top of its double top plate is identical to the left.

## Before you move on

- [ ] Bearing line check: 121½" above the deck at the front end, 97⅛" at the back, 109-5/16"
      at mid-span — checked with a tape.
- [ ] Each rake plate nose is plumb and flush with the front wall's inner face — checked with
      a speed square.
- [ ] Each plate's back end sits flat on the double top plate with no rock — checked by
      sighting and feel.
- [ ] All four stepped studs per side stand plumb, tops tight to the plate underside with no
      daylight — checked with a 4' level and by sighting along the underside.
