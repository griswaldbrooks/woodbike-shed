> **Goal:** each side wall carries a sloped rake plate whose top edge is the roof's bearing
> line, propped on four short stepped studs at the front and on mitred studs behind them, all
> the way to the back corner.
> **Crew:** 1 person · **Time:** about half a day · **Weather:** dry

The roof slopes from the front (high) to the back (low) at **4½ in 12** — it falls 4½" for
every 12" (one foot) of horizontal run. The rake plate's top edge follows that slope: it is
the line the rafters will bear against at the gable ends. Get this line right and the roof
frames itself; get it wrong and every rafter shows it.

Because the roof drops below the side walls' flat plate stack before the back wall, the rake
plate keeps going after the flat plates stop: mitred studs carry it down to the back corner,
where it seats flat on the side wall's double-top-plate corner block.

The stepped studs are short enough that most come out of the offcut pile from wall framing
(see the board packing on the [cut list](r01-cut-list.md)).

Tools: tape, pencil, speed square (or framing square), circular saw and handsaw, 4' level,
ladder or step, string line or straight board long enough to span the wall.

Already built: all four walls standing, plumb, braced, and tied at the plate line
([Stage 6](09-raise-and-brace.md)). On each side wall the flat double top plate (top 97⅛"
above the deck) runs only the front **60¾"** of the wall, and the flat top plate beneath it
runs to **64¾"** — both measured from the front corner. Behind those marks the wall top
follows the roof, and both walls carry their door framing below (left is the MAIN entry,
right the brewery service; the header caps top out 89" above the deck).

## Steps

### 1. Snap the bearing line on each side wall

On the left wall, measure up from the deck at the front end and mark **121½"** on the inside
face of the wall, right where the front wall meets it. At the back corner the line lands
**92⅝"** above the deck, on the side wall's double-top-plate corner block. Snap or
straight-edge a line between the two marks. Mark the right wall the same way.

Check the middle: 32½" in from the front end, the line should sit **109-5/16"** above the
deck. If it does, the slope is right.

The finished wall — door framing included — is grey; the new rake plate, the four stepped studs, and the mitred studs are in colour. The plate's top edge is the bearing line: 121½" above the deck at the front end, falling to 92⅝" at the back corner.](figures/fig-10-rake-wall.svg)

### 2. Cut the rake plates

Take one 6' 10¼" rake plate and offer it up on the wall: nose at the front wall's inner face,
back end reaching the back corner. Trim the front nose with a plumb (vertical) cut so the
nose lands flush with the front wall's inner face. Cut the second plate the same way — the
two walls mirror each other.

> ⚠️ **WARNING** — Circular saw cuts at chest height when the plate is held on the wall ·
> kickback and blade contact · support the plate on sawhorses or the deck, never in the air,
> and keep the cut line clear of your free hand.

The plate lies so its wide face follows the roof slope — its top long edge is the roof's edge
line, and the studs will meet its underside. In the front 60¾" it bears on the flat double
top plate; behind that, on the mitred studs you fit below.

### 3. Stand the front stud and set the plate

Stand the longest stepped stud — the 22¾" one — plumb at the front end of the wall, bottom on
the double top plate, against the front wall. Its top lands just under the bearing line. Rest
the rake plate on it: nose at the front wall's inner face, bedded flat on the double top
plate and the top plate beneath it as far as they run. Brace the plate or have it held, then
fasten the plate down through the flat plates and into the stud top.

### 4. Cut the remaining studs

Cut the three shorter stepped studs per side — **16 31/32", 10 31/32", and 4 31/32"** — with
their tops sloped to match the rake plate's underside. That slope is the roof pitch:
**4½ in 12**, the same 20.6° angle you will use for the rafter birdsmouths
([Stage 8](11-rafters.md)) — set your bevel or speed square once and use it for every mitre
on this page.

Then cut the mitred studs that carry the plate behind the flat plate stack:

- two **6⅜"** and two **5¼"** cripples per side — their bottoms stand on the door header's
  cap, 89" above the deck, in the back bay of the opening;
- two **7' 7⅛"** studs per side for the back-corner zone — bottoms on the bottom plate,
  tops square-cut, carrying the plate's flat seat over the back bearing.

Every mitred top meets the plate underside at the roof pitch; only the bottoms differ. The cut-list
lengths are exact for the modeled positions; cuts land within an eighth, so cut each a hair
long and sneak up on the fit.

### 5. Fit the studs under the plate

Working front to back, stand each stud plumb at its tape reading from the front corner,
toe-fasten it at the bottom, and bring its mitred top tight against the plate underside with
no daylight:

| Stud | Tape reading | Bottom bears on |
|:---|:---|:---|
| stepped — 16 31/32″ | 15½″ | double top plate top (97⅛″) |
| stepped — 10 31/32″ | 31½″ | double top plate top (97⅛″) |
| stepped — 4 31/32″ | 47½″ | double top plate top (97⅛″) |
| header-cap mitre — 6⅜″ | 65½″ | header cap (89″) |
| header-cap mitre — 5¼″ | 68½″ | header cap (89″) |
| back-corner mitre — 7' 7⅛″ | 73½″ | bottom plate |
| back-corner mitre — 7' 7⅛″ | 75½″ | bottom plate |

(The first, 22¾" stepped stud already sits tight against the front wall at tape 0.) Fasten
the plate to each stud top as you go. At the back corner the plate's flat seat bears on the
side wall's double-top-plate corner block — nail through it there too.

Repeat steps 1–5 on the right wall. It is identical: same door framing below, same rake zone
above.

## Before you move on

- [ ] Bearing line check: 121½" above the deck at the front end, 92⅝" at the back corner,
      109-5/16" at 32½" in — checked with a tape.
- [ ] Each rake plate nose is plumb and flush with the front wall's inner face — checked with
      a speed square.
- [ ] Each plate's back end seats flat on the corner block with no rock — checked by sighting
      and feel.
- [ ] All eight studs per side stand plumb, tops tight to the plate underside with no
      daylight — checked with a 4' level and by sighting along the underside.
