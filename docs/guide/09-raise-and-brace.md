# Stage 6 — Raise, tie, brace

> **Goal:** all four walls stand plumb on the deck edges, lapped together at the double top
> plates, held by diagonal bracing that stays on until the siding goes on.
> **Crew:** 2 people minimum — a third helps on the front wall · **Time:** about half a day ·
> **Weather:** calm and dry. Do not raise walls in wind.

This is the heaviest and most dangerous part of the build. A raised front wall is 10' 3" tall and
16' 0" long; once it is off the deck it wants to fall, and it falls fast. Read the whole page
before you lift anything. The warnings sit on the steps that carry the hazard.

## Before you start

- All four walls framed flat, checks passed (Stages 3–5).
- Bracing stock: straight 2×4 offcuts long enough to run diagonally from near the top plate down
  to the deck — at least one per wall, two for the front. Braces are temporary and are **not on
  the lumber order**; cut them from scrap.
- Fasteners are your own, as before (not on either order).
- Tools: 4' level, tape, hammer or driver, stepladder, saw for the braces.
- Pick your helpers and agree on commands before lifting — "lift", "walk", "hold", "down".

## Steps

### 1. Plan the sequence

The walls go up in order: **1 front, 2 back, 3 left, 4 right**. The front wall goes first: it is
the tallest and heaviest, and its full-width double top plate sets the street-side face the side
walls butt into. The back wall goes second; each side wall then rises between the two and ties a
front-to-back pair together. Clear the deck of offcuts, stage the braces where each wall will
stand, and put your crew at the ends of the wall being raised.

![Fig 9.1 — Raising sequence, whole shed in isometric view from above the street-front corner
(Stage 6). Walls rise front, back, left, right; the two-person symbol marks the minimum crew at
each wall.](figures/fig-09-a.svg)

> ⚠️ **WARNING** — The front wall is 10' 3" tall, 16' 0" long, and awkward once it leaves the
> deck. If it comes down it crushes hands and feet and smashes the framing. Two people minimum,
> one at each end; a third in the middle makes the front wall much safer. Do not raise walls in
> more than a light breeze.

### 2. Raise the front wall

One lifter at each end. Crouch, take the top edge, and lift with your legs. As the wall rotates
up, walk toward it hand over hand along the studs, keeping the bottom plate pivoting on the
deck's front edge. Past halfway, keep pushing it up to vertical — **do not let go** — and set it
down with the bottom plate's outer face flush with the deck edge and the wall ends flush with the
deck's side corners.

![Fig 9.2 — Raising the front wall, elevation viewed from the street (Stage 6). The top edge
lifts while the bottom plate pivots on the deck edge; two lifters walk it up hand over hand, and
the dashed outline shows where it lands.](figures/fig-09-b.svg)

> ⚠️ **WARNING** — Never stand under a rising wall, and never hold it overhead with locked arms.
> Keep your back straight and lift with your legs. If the wall starts to go, lower it under
> control — do not fight a falling wall.

### 3. Brace the front wall before anyone lets go

While one person holds the wall plumb — level held on an end stud — the other nails a diagonal
brace on the inside: from a stud near the top plate down to the deck, at about 45°. Nail a second
brace before the helpers step away. A standing wall is not stable until it is braced.

![Fig 9.3 — Bracing a raised wall, elevation of the front wall viewed from inside the shed
(Stage 6). A scrap 2×4 runs diagonally from a stud near the top plate down to the deck, nailed
at both ends, and stays until the siding goes on.](figures/fig-09-c.svg)

### 4. Raise and brace the back wall

Same lift at the back edge: bottom plate outer face flush with the deck's back edge, ends flush
with the deck's side corners. Its 15' 5" double top plate already lies between the two corner
zones — leave it there; the side walls' plates will lap over the corners. Brace it the same way
before letting go.

### 5. Raise and brace the side walls — left, then right

One wrinkle: with the front and back walls standing, the gap between them is 5' 5", and a side
wall measures 5' 8½" across its double-plate lap — it cannot lie flat end-to-end between two
standing walls. Lay the side wall diagonally across the open deck with its front end at its
corner, raise it to vertical in the open with your helper, then walk it over and set its bottom
plate on the deck's side edge. The 3½" double-plate overhang at the back end lands above the back
wall's corner. Nail the side wall's front-end studs into the front wall's corner framing, plumb
it, and brace it. Repeat for the right wall.

### 6. Tie the corners at the double top plates

This is what turns four walls into one box. At each back corner the side wall's double plate
overlaps the back wall, and the back wall's short double plate butts against it from between; at
each front corner the side wall's double plate end butts into the front wall's corner framing,
under the front wall's full-width double plate. Nail through every lap and every butt.

![Fig 9.4 — Corner laps, plan cut horizontally through the double top plates about 8' above the
deck, looking down (Stage 6). Side-wall plates lap over both back corners; the back wall's short
plate fits between them; the front wall's plate runs the full 16' 0".](figures/fig-09-d.svg)

### 7. Plumb everything and leave the braces

Re-check every wall for plumb and nudge it by shifting the foot of its brace. Check the box at
plate level: 16' 0" long, 6' 0" deep, diagonals equal within ½". The bracing stays on until the
siding goes on in Stage 10 — do not remove it for the roof work.

## Before you move on

- [ ] All four walls plumb — checked with the level on an end stud of each wall.
- [ ] Every wall holds at least one diagonal brace, nailed at top and bottom; the front wall
      holds two.
- [ ] Corner laps nailed: both back corners (side plate over back wall) and both front corners
      (side plate end into the front wall's corner).
- [ ] Box measures 16' 0" × 6' 0" at the plate line with diagonals equal within ½" — checked
      with the tape.
- [ ] Bottom plates flush with the deck edges all the way around — checked by eye and tape.
