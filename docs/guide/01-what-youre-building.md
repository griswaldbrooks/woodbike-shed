# What you're building

> **Goal:** know the shed's shape, its key numbers, and what this guide does and does not cover — before you order a single board.

One combined wood shed and bike shed: a 16 ft × 6 ft rectangular box with a single-slope
(shed) roof, the tall wall at the front (street/door) side, three exterior door openings,
1×8 lap siding, and board-and-batten doors. The whole shed rides on two continuous
pressure-treated 4×4 skids bearing on eight Tuff Blocks set in gravel pockets — no concrete
foundation.

## The shape in numbers

| What | Value |
|---|---|
| Framed footprint, wall outer faces | 16′ × 6′ |
| Roof plan, fascia face to fascia face | 18′ × 9′ 3″ |
| Roof overhangs | 24″ at the front, 12″ at the back, 12″ past each side wall |
| Wall height above the deck | front 10′ 3″; back and both sides 8′ 1⅛″ |
| Roof pitch | 4.5 : 12, high side at the front |
| Peak, front fascia top | 11′ 5-11/16″ above the deck |
| Door openings, rough framed | front wall: one 3′ × 7′ and one 6′ × 7′; right wall: one 3′ × 7′ |
| Deck height | deck top sits about 9¾″ above the skid bottoms |
| Modeled parts | 293: 117 framing in 36 cut-list groups, 176 finish |

Every height in this guide is measured up from the deck top — the deck is zero. Horizontal
positions are tape readings from a named edge or corner, never coordinates.

## Key design decisions, in plain language

- **Pre-cut studs.** The back wall and both side walls use standard 92⅝″ pre-cut studs,
  which makes those walls 8′ 1⅛″ tall (stud plus three plates). The front wall is framed to
  10′ 3″ and the roof geometry is derived from it.
- **Two continuous skids.** One full-length 16 ft PT 4×4 per skid line, under the front and
  back wall bearing lines. No splices, no sistering.
- **Birdmouthed rafters.** All 13 rafters carry a flat seat on the front double top plate
  and a second seat on the back double top plate, so each rafter bears flush on both plates
  instead of digging into them.
- **KD SPF #2 framing.** All wall and roof lumber is kiln-dried spruce-pine-fir #2. The
  pressure-treated lumber (ground-contact rated: skids plus the whole floor system) is
  whatever the yard stocks — the species is not yours to choose.
- **Board-and-batten doors.** Four leaves (one single and one center double on the front
  wall, one single on the right wall): vertical 1×6 planks with a 1×4 perimeter frame, hung
  on the casing face with 12″ strap hinges.
- **The siding is the wall surface.** 1×8 lap (drop) siding at 7″ exposure, rabbet-nested so
  the wall stays in one plane. No separate structural sheathing is modeled.
- **Two separate orders, exact quantities.** Framing lumber and finish lumber are ordered
  as completely separate orders, and neither carries any overage.

## What is NOT covered

These are not modeled and are on neither lumber order. If you want them, they are a third,
separate shopping list of your own:

- wall sheathing or weather-resistive barrier
- roof sheathing, underlayment, and roofing material
- fasteners (nails, screws, hinges are the only hardware on any list)
- paint and caulk for the primed finish stock
- door stops and weatherstrip

Do not invent quantities for these from this guide; the orders here contain exactly what
the model builds.

## One known quirk, on purpose

Three front-wall members stand on the deck and run **through** the bottom plate instead of
sitting on top of it: the two 7′ jack studs at the 3-foot door opening, and the 10′ king
stud that stands on the deck right beside that opening's right jack. That is how the model
is built (those three are exempt from its interference checks). In the field: set them
full-length from the deck, and cut the plate sill out from between the two jacks after the
wall stands — the sill segment in a doorway comes out anyway.
