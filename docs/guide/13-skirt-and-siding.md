# Stage 10 — Skirt and lap siding

> **Goal:** every wall is skirted at the deck and skinned with lap siding, from the skirt up to the rake line.
> **Crew:** 1 person · **Time:** about two days · **Weather:** dry — primed boards and fresh cut ends don't like rain

## Before you start

Framing, rake plates, rafters, fascia and rake boards are all up (Stages 1–9). Everything on this
page comes from the **separate finish order** — 1×8 PRIMED. Wall sheathing, roofing, fasteners and
paint are not modeled and are on neither lumber order: supply your own siding nails and plan the
paint separately.

Tools: tape, 4′ level, chalk line, a story pole (a straight 1×2 offcut marked with the course
lines), circular saw or mitre saw, a table saw or ripping guide for the rake cuts and the ripped
top courses, ladder.

## Steps

### 1. Hang the front and back skirt boards

The skirt is the datum for everything you nail after it: all trim starts from its top. Set each
16′ skirt on the rim joist band so its **top edge sits ½″ above the deck top** — that puts its
bottom edge ½″ below the rim joist's own bottom. The skirt sits one layer proud of the wall face:
its face lands where the trim above will land, and the siding comes up behind it. Nail it to the
rim joist band.

skirt layer = trim layer, back on the siding outer face; deck top is the guide's zero -->

![Fig 13.1 — Skirt board at the wall base, section through the front wall viewed from the left
gable end. Stage 10, first member down. The skirt top, ½″ above the deck, is the line every later
board measures from.](figures/fig-13-a.svg)

### 2. Hang the side skirt boards

Set the two 6′ skirts the same way, one on each end wall, same height as the front and back
skirts: top edge ½″ above the deck, square-cut ends at the corners. Check each against the deck
line with the tape before you nail.

### 3. Mark the course lines on every wall

The siding runs at a **7″ exposure**. From the deck top, mark a line every 7″ up each wall — or
mark a story pole once and carry it wall to wall. These lines are the bottom edge of each course.
The first line lands on the deck itself.

### 4. Start the first course behind the skirt

Set the first course with its bottom edge on the deck top, level, full length of its wall. Its
bottom ½″ tucks in behind the skirt. Nail it into the studs.

<!-- model trace: first course z 0..7.5, siding layer; skirt covers z -6.75..0.5 in the trim
layer outboard of it; exposure 7", board width 7-1/2", courses nest via a 1/2" x 3/8" rabbet/tongue -->

The boards are 1×8 drop siding: ¾″ thick, 7½″ wide. Each course has a tongue along its top edge;
the next course's bottom rabbet drops over it, so the courses nest and the wall stays in one
plane — no bevel drift as you climb.

![Fig 13.2 — Two nested siding courses, section on the wall face viewed from the left gable end.
Stage 10, course-on-course. The tongue of the lower course fills the rabbet of the one above;
bottom edges land 7″ apart.](figures/fig-13-b.svg)

### 5. Skin the back wall

Run full 16′ courses up the back wall — 14 courses. No openings: every course is one board. The
rafter tails overhang this wall, so the top course is ripped down to tuck under them: its top
edge lands 95¼″ above the deck, behind where the back frieze will sit. Keep each course's bottom
edge on its 7″ line and nail into the studs.

### 6. Skin the front wall

The front wall has two openings: a 36″ single door (left of centre) and a 72″ double door, both
headed 84″ above the deck.

Below the head line, 12 courses break into three piers each, sized by the openings: 1′ 7″ to the
left of the 36″ door, 3′ 11½″ between the doors, 1′ 5½″ to the right of the 72″ door. Above the
head line, 6 full 16′ courses run wall to wall. The last of those is ripped so its top edge lands
on the plate top, 10′ 3″ above the deck, behind where the front frieze will sit.

> ⚠️ **WARNING** — ladder work · the top courses on the 10′ 3″ front wall put you at height ·
> ladder on firm ground, never reach sideways past the rails

### 7. Skin the side walls up to the rake

**Left wall:** 14 full 6′ courses. The rake line bites into the back end of the top one — it runs
the full 6′ but its top edge comes down to meet the rake at the back.

**Right wall:** this wall carries the third door, headed 84″ above the deck like the front ones.
Every course below the head breaks into two piers: a 1′ piece in front of the door and a 2′ piece
behind it — 13 courses of them. Above the head, one full 6′ course.

On both walls, stop where the courses start meeting the rake line; the next step finishes the top.

### 8. Fit the rake boards on both side walls

Above the last full course, each side wall takes four rake-cut boards. Their top edges are ripped
at the roof pitch — the same angle as the rake boards from Stage 7, about 20½° off square — and
each one butts the tongue top of the course below. Set your bevel from a rake board, not from a
number, and rip each top edge to the line.

Each rake board comes out 20″ shorter than the one below it, measured along the bottom edge —
that falls out of the roof pitch (24⅜″ of rise over a 65″ run) and the 7½″ board width. Cut them
to these bottom-edge lengths, longest to shortest, starting from the bottom of the rake run:

| Wall | Bottom-edge length |
|:---|:---|
| left and right | 5′ 6⅙″ |
| left and right | 3′ 10⅙″ |
| left and right | 2′ 2⅙″ |
| left and right | 6⅙″ |

(⅙″ is not on your tape — cut these a hair long and sneak the rake angle; the frieze covers the
top joints.)

> ⚠️ **WARNING** — ripping angled boards · the sloped top cut is a rip, not a crosscut · keep the
> fence on the straight bottom edge, use a push stick, and never freehand the workpiece past the blade

## Before you move on

- [ ] Skirt top is ½″ above the deck all the way around — checked with tape and level
- [ ] First course level with its bottom edge on the deck top — 4′ level
- [ ] Every course bottom lands on its 7″ line — tape against the story pole
- [ ] The top courses on front and back sit tight against their tuck lines, behind the future frieze — sighted from the ground
- [ ] Rake boards tight to the rake line, top edges parallel to it — tape and bevel gauge
- [ ] Every course fastened into the studs along its length — walk each wall and look for misses
- [ ] No splits at the pier ends and rake tips — look, then fix now while the boards are few
