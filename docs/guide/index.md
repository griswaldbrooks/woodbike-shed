# Start here

This guide builds one shed: a 16 ft × 6 ft wood box with a single-slope roof, the tall
wall at the front, three door openings, lap siding, and board-and-batten doors. It rides
on two 16 ft skids on eight blocks — no concrete. Twelve stages take you from bare ground
to hung doors. One page per stage, in build order. Read this page once, then work the
pages in order.

![Fig. 0.1 — The finished wood bike shed. ISO view from the street, right corner toward you: 16 ft × 6 ft, single-slope roof falling to the back, three board-and-batten doors. This is what exists when Stage 12 is done — every page in this guide builds toward this one picture.](figures/fig-00-finished-shed.svg)

## The datum — read this before any page

- **The deck top is zero.** Every height in this guide is measured up from the top of the
  OSB deck, and says so ("40 inches above the deck").
- **The front is the street side and is the high side.** The roof falls from the front to
  the back. "Left" and "right" are as you stand in the street facing the doors.
- **Horizontal positions are tape readings from a named mark** — "16 inches on centre,
  measured from the left end mark" — never coordinates.
- Parts are named as on the order lists: cut-list name, nominal size, cut length
  ("rafter · 2×6 · 9' 7⅜\"").

## Safety, in four groups

The detailed warnings are printed at the exact step that carries the hazard — these four
groups are the shape of it.

- **Lifting.** The front wall stands 10 ft 3 in tall: two people minimum, every time.
  The 16 ft skids and 18 ft fascia are long and floppy; carry with help and keep fingers
  clear of bearing points.
- **Height.** Stages 7–9 put you ten feet and more above the deck. Firm ladder footing,
  three points of contact, and don't work alone overhead.
- **Cutting.** Birdsmouths, rake cuts and ripped courses mean long saw time. Eye protection
  always; clamp the work; know where the blade exits.
- **Ground work.** Digging and tamping the gravel pockets is gloves-and-eye-protection work;
  crush the stone in lifts, and keep the pocket edges from crumbling under your feet.

## Tools

One list for the whole build; each stage page repeats what it needs so you don't carry this
page up a ladder.

- Tape measure (25 ft) and a pencil
- Speed square and a 4 ft level
- String line — the blocks are set to one common plane, not levelled one by one
- Circular saw; a sharp handsaw or jigsaw for notches
- Drill/driver and a hammer
- Shovel and a hand tamper for the gravel pockets
- Shims ½" and under
- Sawhorses and clamps for wall assembly
- A ladder that reaches past the front plate top

## How to read the figures

Every figure carries a **view badge** in its top-left corner with three facts: the view type
(ELEVATION, PLAN, SECTION or ISO), which way you are looking in building terms ("viewed from
the street"), and a frame anchor — a gravity arrow on elevations and sections, the cut height
on plans and sections, an axis corner on ISO views. Trust the badge before you trust your
first guess; the drawing is drawn to declare its own frame.

Members are drawn in three states: **new work in full colour, already-built work as a grey
outline, future work dashed.** No part is ever drawn alone — every new member appears with
neighbours and a datum edge, and an **arrow to its seat** with a symbol saying how it fastens
(nails, screw, hinge, seat cut). A **red crossed frame** shows a mistake to avoid; a **warning
triangle** marks a safety message.
