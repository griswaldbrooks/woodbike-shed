# Stage 1 — Blocks and skids

> **Goal:** eight blocks set to one level plane, with both continuous 16-foot skids bearing on them, square and aligned.
> **Crew:** 1 person · **Time:** about a day · **Weather:** dry ground

Left and right in this guide are as you stand in the street facing the shed. FRONT is the street
side — the tall wall with the doors. Your tape zero for this whole stage is the **left-end corner**
of the footprint.

## Before you start

Parts tick-off (from the framing order, [P02](02-order-framing.md)):

| Qty | Part (cut-list name) | Lumber | Cut length |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | skid | 4×4 PT, ground contact | 16' 0" |

Foundation materials — **not on either lumber order** (see [P02](02-order-framing.md)):

- 8 Tuff Blocks
- 8 concrete pavers, 16–18" (load spreaders)
- about 1 yd³ of ¾" **angular** crushed stone
- landscape fabric
- shims — used only where needed, never more than ½" total at any one spot

Tools: shovel, probing rod (rebar or similar), tape, string line + line level (or a transit),
hand tamper, a 2' level, marking paint or stakes.

The two skids are single 16-foot 4×4s — no splices, no sistered joints. If your yard delivered
shorter stock, stop and sort the order before digging anything.

## Steps

### 1. Get buried utilities marked

> ⚠️ **WARNING** — buried utilities · a shovel through a line can electrocute, explode, or cut service · call your utility locator before any digging (in the US: call 811, free), and wait for the marks.

### 2. Mark the footprint and the two skid lines

Mark a 16' × 6' rectangle on the ground with the front edge facing the street, and square the
corners (3‑4‑5 method, then check that both diagonals are equal). Inside it, mark the two skid
lines: one under the front wall, one under the back wall. Each skid's centerline runs 1¾" in from
its shed edge, so the skid's outside face lands flush with the footprint edge. The two centerlines
are 5' 8½" apart, measured across the shed.

Along each skid line, mark the four pocket centers: **6", 5' 6", 10' 6", and 15' 6"** on the tape
from the left-end corner. That puts them 6" in from each skid end and 5' apart — equal spacing for
the continuous 16-foot skid.

Stage 1, before digging: eight gravel pockets in two rows of four, a paver and a Tuff Block in each, and the two skid lines dashed where the skids will land. Every position is a tape reading from the left-end corner.](figures/fig-04-a.svg)

### 3. Dig the eight pockets down to firm soil

Dig each pocket at least 24" square, centered on your marks, down to firm subsoil. Probe first to
find how deep firm soil is — depth varies across the footprint, typically 8–12". Dig to firm soil,
not to a fixed depth. Keep each pocket top at or above finished grade, and crown the surrounding
grade so water falls away from the shed.

![Fig 4.2 — One gravel pocket in section, cut across the front skid at a block (line B–B in Fig 4.1, viewed toward the right end). Bottom to top: firm soil, tamped gravel, paver, block — and above, dashed, the skid and floor framing that land on it later. Dig to firm soil, never to a fixed depth; shim only at the block top, never more than ½".](figures/fig-04-b.svg)

### 4. Line the pockets and fill with tamped gravel

Line each pocket with landscape fabric, lapped up the sides — it stops the stone migrating into
soft soil. Fill with ¾" angular crushed stone in 3" lifts, tamping each lift before adding the
next. Bring the stone up to the pocket top.

### 5. Set the pavers

Set one concrete paver flat on the tamped gravel in each pocket, centered on the mark. The paver
spreads the shed's load across the whole pocket.

> ⚠️ **WARNING** — heavy lifting · a 16–18" concrete paver can weigh over 60 lb and pin fingers · lift with your legs, keep hands clear when setting down, get help if the carry is awkward.

### 6. Set the blocks to ONE plane with a string line

Run string lines along the two skid rows and level the strings to each other, so they define one
common plane across all eight pockets. Set a Tuff Block on each paver and bring its top to the
string. Do not level the blocks one by one — the plane is what matters; every block top meets the
same string. If a block is low, pack gravel under its paver; if high, re-seat it. **Shim only at
the block top, and never more than ½" in total at any spot.**

### 7. Set the skids on the block rows

Lay the front skid on the front row of blocks, under the front-wall bearing line, and the back
skid on the back row. Each skid bears on its four blocks, ends flush with the left and right ends
of the footprint — the rim joists will land flush on those same ends next stage. Align each skid's
outside face with the footprint edge. Shim between block and skid only where needed, ½" or less,
and bring both skids to the string-line plane.

### 8. Check the rectangle

Measure diagonally across the four outside corners of the two skids, corner to opposite corner.
The two diagonals must be equal — each runs about 17' 1". If they differ, shift the skids in their
blocks until they match. Nothing downstream can be squared if this rectangle is not.

## Before you move on

- [ ] All eight block tops on the one string-line plane — checked by sighting down each string; no shim stack over ½"
- [ ] Skid centerlines 5' 8½" apart, measured across the shed — checked with a tape
- [ ] Each skid's outside face flush with the footprint edge, ends flush left and right — checked with a tape
- [ ] Diagonals across the skid corners equal within ½", each about 17' 1" — checked with a tape
- [ ] Pocket tops at or above grade, ground crowned away from the shed — checked by eye after wetting the ground
