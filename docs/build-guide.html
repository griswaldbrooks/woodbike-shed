1. Project overview

One combined wood shed + bike shed: a 16 ft × 6 ft rectangular box with a single-slope (shed) roof, the tall wall at the front (door) side, three exterior door openings, lap siding, and board-and-batten doors. It rides on two continuous pressure-treated 4×4 skids bearing on eight Tuff Blocks set in gravel pockets — no concrete foundation.

Key design decisions (plain language)

  • 92‑5/8" pre-cut studs. Back, left and right walls use standard pre-cut studs; the wall height is 97⅛" (was 97½" with the old 93" studs). The roof was re-derived about the unchanged front wall, moving the pitch from 24/65 to 24.375/65.
  • Two continuous 16‑ft 4×4 skids. No sistering, no splices: one PT 4×4 per skid line, full shed length, under the front and back wall bearing lines. Hingham Lumber stocks 16' 4×4s.
  • Birdmouth-seated rafters. Every one of the 13 rafters carries a front birdsmouth (flat seat on the front double top plate top at z = 123", heel 4" outboard of the wall inner face) and a back seat on the back double top plate top (z = 97⅛"), so the rafters bear flush on both plates instead of interpenetrating them.
  • SPF #2 kiln-dried framing. All wall and roof lumber is KD SPF #2. Pressure-treated lumber (ground-contact rated, skids + entire floor system) is whatever Hingham Lumber stocks — the species is not ours to choose.
  • Board-and-batten doors. Four leaves (one single + one center double on the front wall, one single on the right wall): vertical 1×6 planks with a 1×4 perimeter frame, hung on the casing face with 12" strap hinges.
  • 1×8 lap (drop) siding, 7" exposure. Rabbet-nested courses keep the wall in one plane; no separate structural sheathing is modeled — the siding is the wall surface.
  • Two separate order lists. Framing lumber and finish lumber (siding/trim/doors/hardware) are ordered as completely separate orders — never merged. Quantities are EXACT (no waste factor); shortages get a follow-up run.

2. Materials and ordering

Yard: Hingham Lumber. KD framing species is SPF #2; the yard stocks 16' 2×4, 16' 4×4 and true 20' 2×6. PT ground-contact species is whatever the yard carries (not our choice). Quantities are EXACT — 0% overage per the captain's decision: plan a follow-up run for shortages instead of padding the order. Saw kerf allowance used for stock packing: ⅛" per cut.
Framing and finish are TWO SEPARATE ORDERS. The finish lumber (siding, trim, doors, hardware) is modeled for real but ordered on a completely separate list — never mix the two. They are presented separately below, mirroring order_list.csv and order_list_finish.csv.

3. Step-by-step build guide

Work top to bottom: Stage 1 foundation/skids/floor → Stage 2 walls (front, back, left, right) → Stage 3 roof → Stage 4 finish. Every parts table lists model part names, counts and cut lengths exactly as they appear in CUT_LIST.md. Model coordinates: X along the 16' length, Y across the 6' width (front wall at low Y), Z up; deck top = z 0.

Foundation materials (separate from the lumber orders): 8 Tuff Blocks, 8 pavers 16–18", ~1 yd³ ¾" crushed stone, landscape fabric, shims ≤ ½".

1.1 Gravel pockets and blocks

Eight gravel pockets — four per skid line — carry the whole shed. Dig each pocket at least 24" square down to firm subsoil (depth varies; typically 8–12"), line with landscape fabric, fill with ¾" angular crushed stone in 3" lifts tamping each lift, set a concrete paver, then a Tuff Block. Set all eight block tops to ONE common plane with a string line, not individual level readings; shim only under ½". Keep pocket tops at or above finished grade and crown the grade away from the shed.

1.2 Skids

  1. Set the two 16' PT 4×4 skids on the block rows: the front skid under the front-wall bearing line, the back skid under the back-wall bearing line. Skid centerlines are 5' 8½" apart (68.5"); each skid is the full 192" shed length — ends land flush with the future rim joist ends (model x −3.5…188.5).
  2. Each skid bears on four blocks, 6" in from each end and 5' apart (Fig. 2). Level and align the skids to the string-line plane; shim ≤ ½" only.
  3. Check the rectangle: equal diagonals across the two skid lines before framing starts.

1.3 Floor framing and decking

  1. Nail the two 16' PT 2×6 rim joists flat-side out along the skid outsides (front rim y −3.5…−2, back rim y 67…68.5), bearing on the skids (rim bottoms at skid tops, z −6.25).
  2. Set the 14 PT 2×6 floor joists at 69" between rims, on the layout below: first joist flush at the left end (x −3.5), then 16" o.c. (x 11, 27, 43, 59, 75, 91…), a doubled pair at x 91 + 92.5 (keeps the OSB seam and adds stiffness mid-span), 16" o.c. again (108.5, 124.5, 140.5, 156.5, 172.5), last joist flush at the right end (x 187).
  3. Deck with the four ¾" OSB half-sheets (72" × 48"): sheet edges land on joists at x = 44.5, 92.5, 140.5; sheets run the full 72" width. Deck top is the build datum z = 0 — every wall measurement in the stages below starts here.

Stage 2 — Walls

Four wall assemblies, framed flat on the deck and stood in this order: front (tall, 123"), back, left, right — both side walls are the shed's rake walls (their double top plates and roof-edge studs carry the sloped roof line; the sloped rake plates themselves land in Stage 3). All wall lumber is 2×4 KD SPF #2. Deck top (z 0) is the reference plane for every height.

Sheathing: the model has NO separate wall sheathing — the 1×8 lap siding (Stage 4) is the wall surface, applied directly over the framing. If you want structural sheathing or a weather-resistive barrier, that is an addition outside the model and the order lists; the stages below assume the as-modeled build. Temporary diagonal bracing holds each wall plumb until the siding goes on.

  1. 3.2 Rafters with birdsmouth seats

  1. Mark the rafter layout on the double top plates: 13 rafters at 15⅞" o.c., first at the left rake wall (x −3.5), last at the right rake wall (x 187).
  2. Cut all 13 rafters identical from one pattern: front birdsmouth — flat seat on the front DTP top (z 123"), heel 4" outboard of the front wall inner face (y −4.0), plumb kick at the inner face; back birdsmouth — flat seat on the back DTP top (z 97⅛") across the plate width out to the back wall outer face (y 68.5), plumb kick there; plumb tail cuts at both ends (front tail 24" past the front wall face, back tail 12" past the back wall face).
  3. Seat each rafter on both plates, toe-nail/screw through the plate. The rafter tops define the roof plane; tail tops land flush with the fascia tops (137‑11/16" front, 97‑3/16" back).

3.3 Fascia and rake boards

  1. Nail the two 18' 2×6 fascia to the rafter tails: front fascia at the front tails (z 132‑3/16…137‑11/16), back fascia at the back tails (z 91‑11/16…97‑3/16), each running x −15.5…200.5 — 12" past each side wall. Rafter tail tops come out flush with the fascia tops.
  2. Fit the two rake boards (same 115.344" parallelogram as a rafter, no seats) along the left and right roof edges on the gable overhangs, butting the fascia inner faces.

Stage 4 — Finish (siding, trim, doors)

All finish lumber is on the separate finish order: 1×8 PRIMED lap siding, PRIMED 1×6/1×10 trim, KD 1×4/1×6 doors; hardware is line items only. The model builds the finish in layers, each proud of the one behind: framing → siding (¾") → trim (¾") → door planks (¾") → door frames (¾"). Work in that order.
